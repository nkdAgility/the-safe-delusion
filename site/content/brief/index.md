Six organisations adopted the Scaled Agile Framework. This is what happened next, in the terms a board measures.

Every line below is drawn from the guide, which carries the full account and the original sources.

---

## ANZ Banking Group

Rolled out from 2016, expanded across the Australian division from 2017 under a mandate to deliver faster and underpin the bank's mortgage business.

Five years on, its home loan approvals were among the slowest in Australia. The bank lost substantial share of a $2 trillion mortgage market. Its share price was down 17% across the CEO's seven-year tenure.

An industry analyst's assessment: not an agile organisation, and *"quite sluggish in terms of some of the things that they're doing."*

[The full account →](/safe-decision-makers/#anz-bank-initiatives-for-scaling-agile)

## Capital One

Scaled Agile published Capital One as a customer success story in 2017.

In January 2023 the company eliminated all 1,100 of its Agile roles. The announcement made clear the practice had remained siloed inside IT without the business involvement the programme had been started to achieve.

The case study remained published.

[The full account →](/safe-decision-makers/#capital-one)

## Peaksys

Adopted around 2020 to support entry into a new market.

Time from prioritising a feature to activating it for customers ran to approximately **one year**. Work in progress and multitasking rose. Quarterly commitment cycles could not accommodate a fast-moving product bet.

They concluded the framework was holding them back and stopped.

[The full account →](/safe-decision-makers/#peaksys)

## Volvo Cars

Two and a half years of transformation, from 2017 to the end of 2019, across an organisation employing around 10,000 people in software.

By September 2022 a department of eleven teams and just over 100 people had dropped it. Their assessment: the quarterly planning event had become largely presentational, and the coordination artefact it produced added very little value against the effort it consumed. The real work was organised elsewhere.

[The full account →](/safe-decision-makers/#volvo-cars)

## Fitbit

Adopted between 2015 and 2016 and promoted by Scaled Agile as a success.

The financial performance cited in that case study is contradicted by the company's public financial data. Fitbit later abandoned the framework, and the person who had championed it left the company.

The case study remained listed as a success story.

[The full account →](/safe-decision-makers/#fitbit)

## United States Air Force

In December 2019 the Chief Software Officer issued a memorandum strongly discouraging rigid, prescriptive frameworks, naming SAFe.

Scaled Agile publicly offered free consulting to address the concerns. The USAF subsequently confirmed the memorandum's conclusions stood, and that the framework would not be used in any form in its DevSecOps programme.

[The full account →](/safe-decision-makers/#us-air-force)

## What the evidence adds up to

Across every case that permits independent verification, the guide finds **no evidence of lasting benefit** against the time and money spent — and finds that the weaknesses of the pre-Agile operating models these organisations were trying to leave behind were amplified rather than reduced.

Three of the cases above were published or showcased as successes.

The organisations that recovered did so after stopping. Beijer Electronics left in June 2021 and improvement followed. Peaksys stopped. The Volvo department dropped it and worked more fluidly without it.

---

## Before you commit further

Three questions worth putting to whoever is accountable for the programme:

1. **How long does it currently take us to get one decision in front of a customer?** Peaksys measured a year and treated that as the finding.
2. **Are we removing dependencies between teams, or scheduling around them?** The two produce very different cost curves, and only one compounds in your favour.
3. **What would we expect to see if this were not working?** If the answer is *"we would need more time and more training"*, that condition cannot be failed.
